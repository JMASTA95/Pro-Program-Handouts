Ozone therapy is training, not supplementation

If you already train, you understand the whole mechanism. Ozone therapy is a hormetic stimulus — a small, controlled stress that your body answers by building capacity. It is not an antioxidant you take; it is the opposite. It briefly lowers your antioxidant status so that your cells manufacture more of their own. The session is the workout. The adaptation over the following days is the result.

What your body builds afterward

Endogenous antioxidant capacity

Messenger molecules activate Nrf2, and over the following hours your cells increase their own machinery — SOD, catalase, glutathione peroxidase, HO-1, and the glutathione systems. You end up with a higher ceiling than you started with.

Oxygen efficiency

The same conditioning signal improves how efficiently tissues handle oxygen — the metabolic side of the response, and the reason this is used around training load and altitude rather than only in illness.

Lower inflammatory tone

The pulse shifts the Nrf2 / NF-κB balance toward anti-inflammatory activity. For most people chasing recovery and longevity, resting inflammatory tone is the variable that actually moves how they feel.

Joints, arthritis & recovery from load

Joints are the best-measured territory for people who load their bodies hard. Ozone therapy given before a stress makes tissue more resistant to it, and given into an injured area it restarts stalled repair — improving local oxygenation and blood flow, normalizing inflammation, activating stem cells, and switching on collagen genes. On the performance side, footballers treated twice weekly for five weeks raised VO2max 28% and maximum running time 20%, against 12% and 6% untreated.

Rheumatoid arthritis

RCT, N=60. Ozone therapy added to methotrexate produced a greater clinical response than the drug alone, with lower anti-CCP and oxidative-damage markers and higher antioxidant capacity.

Knee osteoarthritis

In 238 knee-pain patients, ozone therapy gave faster and superior short-term relief than hyaluronic acid or platelet-rich plasma, with gains in pain, stiffness, and function at two months. A systematic review across 858 patients found it safe and effective for pain.

Tendon, disc & soft tissue

In chronic tennis elbow, ozone therapy beat corticosteroid at 3, 6, and 9 months. In 109 low-back-pain patients, 79% had significant reductions in pain and disability. In hamstring injuries it was well tolerated, with improved blood flow and better function.

Preconditioning is the mechanism behind the recovery effect: before knee arthroscopy, ozone therapy raised plasma glutathione roughly 89% and lowered malondialdehyde, a marker of oxidative damage, about 35% — the same insult pattern that underlies hard eccentric loading and joint trauma.
Frequency is the whole program

Adaptation has a time constant of roughly 24 to 72 hours. Land the next session while capacity is still elevated and you start from a higher baseline; the gains ratchet upward over weeks. Space sessions too far apart and each one resolves before the next arrives — you pay a re-priming tax and never accumulate anything. This is exactly the logic of a training block, and it is the entire argument for treating at home: nobody builds an adaptation on a schedule set by appointment availability.

What protocols look like

Across 138 randomized trials, protocols cluster around every-other-day to three-times-weekly loading, then weekly to twice-weekly maintenance. A common rectal pattern is daily for three weeks, then a week off. There is always cycling.

Overreaching is real here too

Sessions stacked too close, or a dose too high for you, means recovery from the stimulus never completes — and stress accumulates instead of adaptation. Deload weeks are part of the dose, not a pause from it.

Running it well

Set expectations correctly

Most of the evidence base is in disease populations, where there was dysfunction to correct. If your inflammatory tone is already low and your recovery is already good, expect a smaller effect than the literature suggests.

Judge it on things you can actually track over a block — sleep quality, resting heart rate and HRV, session-to-session recovery — not on how any single session felt.

Talking to your clinician

  1. Start low and hold it. The temptation to jump concentration is the most common way people convert a stimulus into a stressor.
  2. Set a loading block, a maintenance frequency, and a scheduled week off before you begin.
  3. If you compete, check ozone therapy against your governing body's rules — this document is not an anti-doping clearance.
  4. Never inhale ozone gas. Report lingering fatigue — that is the signal to back the dose down, not push through.
